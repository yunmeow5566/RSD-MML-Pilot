# RSD-MML-Pilot
## Overview
This is a student-facing repository for a high school computer science course.

- RSD stands for Riverview School District, located in Duvall, WA.

- MML is a brief alias for a course name, Introduction for Modern Machline Learning and Applications.（It might be updated to something including Python) 

The primary programming language in this course and repository will be Python.

## Expectations for Students
I have adopted the T.R.I.C.K. education philosophy (Trust, Respect, Independence, Collaboration, and Kindness) from Ms. Esther Wojcicki with my own implementation. 
- Trust: I believe that everyone in this class is eager to learn and spend time wisely. That's why I want to listen to your learning goals in the beginning of the school year so we can set right expectations for both sides.
- Respect: like what I have been doing for my 3rd graders in a Saturday Mandarin school, I always call students as 'gentlemen', 'ladies' or 同學 in Mandarin, not 'kid', 'boy' or 'girl'. I respect everyone's time and efforts for this class: I will do my part to take feedback openly and seriously, while I expect students to work with me like partners. 
- Independence: My ultimate goal is to grow every student to be a confident and independent learner. When something unexpected happens, you will develop a strategy to unblock yourself. I am fully aware of that some information or suggestions can unblock students immediately while it might take weeks for students to build that mental model or big overview, that's where I will try to ask some questions to guide you through. 
- Collaboration: Modern software development takes hundreds of people with diverse background and expertise to make a solid product. Through this class, you will work with your peers in classroom discussion and projects. You might have disagreements or conflict with your team members, and you will be expected to identify these conflicts and work with your team to resolve them.
- Kindness: Like I will treat you as how I want to be treated as a student, please treat others as how you want to be treated. 

## Grading Categories
- Final project: including related planning discussions, reports, presentations and all the source code. 
- Homework assignment: take-home assignments for course units, programming assignments and reports committed to Github. 
- Participation: attendance, classroom behaviors, in-class assignments (in Github or papers), peer review and discussion. 
- Quiz: there will be a few in-class quizzes related to safety practices. 
- Leadership: including but not limited to: (1) participate in TSA or other organizations, (2) contribute to course content through constructive feedback and/or Github issues or pull requests to the course repository (outside of course assignments), (3) volunteer to be a 'Github' master or 'VSCode' master to support your peers.
